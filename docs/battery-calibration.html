Battery Calibration Guide

ASUS ROG Flow Z13 (GZ302EA) · Ryzen AI MAX+ 395 · Bazzite 44 · Li-ion 70 Wh (design)

BAT0 · ACPI smart battery baseline 62.83 / 70.0 Wh (89.7 %) cycle count 0 charge limit: 100 % (lifted for calibration) 80 % boot-timer: disabled during calibration

Why recalibrate

The battery reports 62.8 Wh full capacity out of a 70 Wh design at 0 charge cycles — a healthy battery should read close to design. The cause is almost certainly a stale fuel-gauge reading: the machine has spent its life charging only to the 80 % limit, so the gauge has never observed a full 0–100 % swing and its learned "full" value has drifted down. One or two complete discharge → charge cycles lets the gauge re-learn the true endpoints and typically recovers most of the missing capacity.

If you start this guide later (fresh boot), re-run the two preparation commands above first.

01 Cycle 1 — full charge

1

Plug in AC and charge to 100 %

Watch it reach STATUS=Full / CAPACITY=100:

watch -n5 'grep -E "STATUS|CAPACITY|ENERGY_FULL" /sys/class/power_supply/BAT0/uevent'
2

Leave it plugged in 1–2 h after reaching 100 %

Allows top-balancing and gives the gauge time to register the full point.

02 Cycle 1 — calibration discharge

3

Unplug and use normally until it auto-suspends

GNOME force-suspends at critical battery (~3–5 %) — that is the natural deep-discharge endpoint. No need to drain below that; deep-draining to literal zero is unnecessary and hard on the cell.

4

Optional deeper drain

Leave the machine suspended for 1–2 h before recharging — it trickles down a little further, which can help the gauge. Do not leave it at 0 % for long.

03 Cycle 1 — recharge & check

5

Recharge to 100 % uninterrupted

Plug in and let it complete to Full, then leave it plugged for a while longer. Important: if you reboot during this phase, re-run charge-limit 100 after login — the 80 % timer is disabled, but the EC limit persists at whatever it was last set to.

6

Record the result

grep ENERGY_FULL /sys/class/power_supply/BAT0/uevent
echo "2026-… (after cycle 1): $(grep ENERGY_FULL= /sys/class/power_supply/BAT0/uevent)" >> ~/.cache/battery-calibration.log

Note: ENERGY_FULL sometimes only updates on the first discharge after a full charge — if it hasn't moved yet, unplug, use it for a few minutes, and re-read.

04 Assessment

Reading after calibrationMeaning
≥ ~66 Wh (> 94 %)Success — gauge re-learned; battery healthy
63–66 Wh (90–94 %)Partial improvement — run one more full cycle (repeat §01–03)
Stays ~63 Wh after 2 good cyclesThis is the battery's real usable capacity — accept it (design Wh includes manufacturer reserve; 0 cycles rules out wear)

05 Restore normal operation

charge-limit 80
systemctl --user enable --now charge-limit-80.timer
charge-limit                    # sanity check → "battery charge limit: 80%"

Expected normal behavior afterwards

The 80 % limit and boot-timer resume protecting the battery on AC. When you want a full charge (e.g. before a trip): charge-limit 100 — the next boot's timer automatically returns the limit to 80 %. The panel-refresh service (60 Hz on battery) is unrelated to charging and keeps working.

06 Safety & troubleshooting

Safety

  • Calibration is occasional maintenance — near-empty discharges are fine every few months, not as a daily habit.
  • Never store the machine at 0 % — recharge promptly after the discharge phase.
  • Keep AC plugged and don't interrupt charging mid-way for no reason (harmless, just slows the gauge update).
  • This machine's battery is internal; no user-accessible removal needed for calibration.
